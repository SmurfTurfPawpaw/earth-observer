# Intelligence source policy

Reviewed 11 October 2026. The panel discovers public reports and links to their original sources. It does not copy article bodies, photographs, maps, or publisher summaries, and it does not certify reported claims. No user photos or notes are sent to these providers.

## Baseline integrations

- **The GDELT Project — scheduled GKG 2.1 topic mentions.** The [official codebook](https://data.gdeltproject.org/documentation/GDELT-Global_Knowledge_Graph_Codebook-V2.1.pdf) documents WEB source identifiers, themes and geographic mentions. [GDELT's terms](https://www.gdeltproject.org/about.html) permit use and redistribution with a project citation and website link. The default sample exposes original source links, reviewed machine topic labels and countries mentioned, with batch/retrieval times. Publisher article content, quotes and images are never fetched or republished. FIPS geography is explicitly crosswalked to current ISO codes; country mentions do not establish incident locations or publisher nationality. Original publication/event dates remain unavailable. The optional [DOC 2.0 Article List](https://blog.gdeltproject.org/gdelt-doc-2-0-api-debuts/) extension supplies bounded headline/link metadata only when explicitly enabled; its `seendate` remains an indexing date.
- **U.S. Geological Survey — M4.5+ earthquakes, past seven days.** [Official GeoJSON feed documentation](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/feed/v1.0/geojson.php) describes origin, update and generation times and links to individual event reports. [USGS copyright guidance](https://www.usgs.gov/information-policies-and-instructions/copyrights-and-credits) explains the public-domain status of USGS-produced information and exceptions for credited third-party material. Only USGS measurement metadata is used here, with attribution; no third-party photographs or documents are copied. Origin time is shown as an event time, review status can change, and this feed is omitted under a country filter because it supplies no authoritative country codes.
- **Global Disaster Alert and Coordination System, GDACS — recent disaster event metadata.** The [official API quick start](https://www.gdacs.org/Documents/2025/GDACS_API_quickstart_v1.pdf) states that data are free through its APIs and requires source acknowledgement. The [March 2025 terms](https://www.gdacs.org/Documents/2025/GDACS_Terms_of_use_Mar_25.pdf) explain that modeled or automatic notifications may contain errors, may need validation, and do not replace alerts issued by national authorities. Metadata carries the GDACS attribution and retains reported event times, modification time, location and affected-country codes. Locations may be representative centroids. Every GDACS card identifies it as a modeled/automatic report, and links to the original event report.

- **NASA EONET — curated natural-event references.** One fixed [v3 query](https://eonet.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/v3) selects wildfires, severe storms and volcanoes within its seven-day query and 60-event ceiling. [EONET's purpose and disclaimer](https://eonet.gsfc.nasa.gov/what-is-eonet) describe visualization and general information while qualifying approximate spatial and temporal extents. Cards preserve EONET geometry/closed dates, retrieval time and referenced-source links; these are not verified incident or publication times. No geometry coordinates, linked articles, photos or descriptions are collected. [Curation](https://eonet.gsfc.nasa.gov/event-curation) can share GDACS or other underlying sources, so these cards never form independent-confirmation groups. NASA's [data guidance](https://science.data.nasa.gov/about/license) does not license every linked publisher. The application admits this reviewed metadata-display purpose, not a blanket CC0 claim.

## Request and freshness behavior

The independent laptop collector acquires one fixed bounded EONET query directly, alongside USGS and GDACS, without waiting for a visitor to open a panel. It prepares bounded metadata before authenticated publication to the hosted R2/D1 storage. EONET titles, category IDs, original geometry/closed dates and source links remain separate from verified incidents. Country filters omit its metadata because it supplies no admitted authoritative country codes. The [EONET scheduled artifact](EONET_SNAPSHOTS.md) remains a compatibility path for deployments using the owned release; it is not the active laptop acquisition path. Outbound source links are never followed automatically.

Production panel reads consume prepared hosted metadata and do not acquire these four upstream sources. The laptop process remains active independently of browser sessions; sleeping or disconnected hardware still interrupts collection. The continuous-delivery candidate acquires GDELT's fixed latest GKG manifest/archive locally, with ZIP integrity and compressed/expanded/row limits, a CPU worker and retained retry state. Existing hosted adapters retain the [owned snapshot](GDELT_SNAPSHOTS.md) compatibility path. Both paths preserve generation, retrieval and batch dates, expire individual records after 24 hours, and disclose sampled gaps. They do not provide exhaustive article crawling or historical backfill.

Country filters match countries mentioned in GKG metadata, while topic filters inspect reviewed labels and publisher domains in the bounded sample. They do not issue extra GDELT searches or inspect article bodies. A country mention is not an incident location or publisher country. Empty results do not establish that no reports exist. Topic labels cannot be treated as real headlines or corroboration.

The active direct metadata adapters use bounded minute-level checks, shared requests and provider backoff; a successful check does not imply a new observation. GDELT publishes discrete batches, and EONET is curated metadata. Failures retain last-good data with its original dates and a stale reason, within its retention limit. `GDELT_LIVE_FALLBACK=1` remains an optional bounded headline extension absent from the public baseline.

Only opened, visible panels read displayed data; collection runs independently. The continuous-delivery candidate subscribes browsers to durable collection revisions and reads changed prepared data when notified, with periodic recovery only when the stream is disconnected or unsupported. Timekeeping is independent of network acquisition. Hidden tabs and closed panels release their browser work. Topic input remains local and bounded, without arbitrary upstream URLs/operators. News searches cover the retained 24-hour sample; hazard query scope is seven days. Neither establishes complete global coverage.

## Related coverage

GKG topic mentions never group by their generated labels. Other records may group close earthquake coordinates/time or similar original headline words/time, retaining each link separately. The group label is **indicative related coverage**, never confirmation or a confidence score. GDACS and USGS may share measurements; articles may be syndicated or share underlying sources. Search matches and grouped links must not be interpreted as independent corroboration.

## Dated research context

Opening news also loads one bounded, same-origin research JSON file. Its three dated checks link to five official records and label paraphrases, creator-comparison inferences, review dates and expired authorizations separately from live feed groups. Filtering is local; this reference set has no polling or demand counter. An optional validated device copy lasts at most seven days from retrieval and retains the original review dates. See [primary review](RESEARCH_PRIMARY_REVIEW.md) for exact coverage: it adds no official transcript or audio verification and does not complete the ten-month Maxinomics research request.

## Deferred integration

ReliefWeb is excluded from the baseline. Its [current request documentation](https://apidoc.reliefweb.int/parameters) requires a pre-approved application name from 1 November 2025. Its [API terms](https://apidoc.reliefweb.int/#terms-of-service) also note that contributed reports can retain their original sources' copyrights. A future integration requires approved credentials and a metadata-only scope; no arbitrary application name is assumed to be authorized.
